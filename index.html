How it works

Three steps after every trip. The split and each man's shares are set up once from your crew agreement and remembered for the next trip.

  1. Snap the paperwork

    Fish tickets, fuel and bait receipts, invoices and lost gear. The app reads species, pounds, prices and totals from the photo, or you type them in.

  2. Check the numbers

    Every scanned number is checked against the photo before it counts. The app flags possible duplicate expenses and reconciles everything to the cent.

  3. Send the slips

    The owner approves, and each crew member gets a slip showing his share, his deductions and the tickets and receipts behind it.

Nothing hidden. For the crew or the skipper.

Settlement disputes usually come from numbers nobody can see. Here, everyone can check the same thing.

  • Every number has its paper. Crew can open the fish ticket or receipt behind each line once the settlement is ready.
  • Changes are logged. If an expense or a ticket changes after the settlement, the old and new amounts are shown. Nothing is changed quietly.
  • Personal deductions stay personal. Advances, license and gear come off one man's pay, not the crew share.
  • An honest skipper has proof. The same record that protects the crew protects the boat.

What it handles

  • Several fish tickets and species per trip
  • Expenses charged before the split, to the owner only, to the crew only, or by your own percentage
  • Lost gear: groundline, anchors, buoy line, buoys
  • Full and partial shares, advances and personal deductions
  • Warnings for possible duplicate expenses
  • A year-end 1099 preparation report to review with your accountant. The app does not file with the IRS.

Set up for your crew agreement

Settlement rules differ from boat to boat. The split, how each expense is charged, and how shares are set must follow your own crew agreement, and the app is set up to match it. Tax questions belong with a qualified US accountant.

Questions skippers ask

What if the scan reads a number wrong?

Every scanned number is shown next to the photo and has to be confirmed before it counts. You can correct anything, or type the ticket in by hand.

Who sees my catch data?

Only people on your boat that you invite: owner, skipper, bookkeeper and crew. Crew see their settlement after the trip is landed and settled. Data is never shared with other boats and never sold, and no fishing locations are stored.

Do I have to change how we split the catch?

No. The split, how each expense is charged, and each man's shares are set up to match your own crew agreement.

Does it file my 1099s?

No. It adds up each crew member's shares through the year and gives you a preparation report to review with your accountant, who files the forms.

Does it work offline at sea?

Not yet. Today you need a connection to add tickets and receipts. Offline use is planned, and pilot feedback decides how it should work.

What does it cost after the pilot?

The pilot is free through the pilot season. After that, founding pilot boats pay $25 a month (or $250 a year), locked for as long as they stay. Regular pricing is expected to be around $40–50 per boat per month at launch. Founding boats keep their price either way. Nothing is charged without your agreement.

What if I stop using it?

You can export everything to a spreadsheet at any time. During the pilot there is no subscription to cancel.
